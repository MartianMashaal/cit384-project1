City design

Reading a city at full speed

A game city has two jobs: it has to create a believable place and help the player understand where to go. These four worlds balance those jobs in very different ways.

Mirror's Edge

White space and one loud color

The city's white walls and blue sky make every red door, pipe, and ramp impossible to miss. Red does not mean decoration here. It is a sentence: “you can move through this.” The limited palette also gives the city its sterile, controlled personality.

Remember Me

A future that remembers its past

Neo-Paris works because familiar streets have not been erased. Balconies, stone facades, and older neighborhoods remain underneath the technology. The memory effects use warmer light and circular forms, so they feel separate from the physical city without looking disconnected from it.

#2A2145#674783#C96C45#F4BC76#293D55

Cyberpunk 2077

Density tells the story

Night City rarely gives the eye an empty surface. Signs cover buildings, freeways cut between towers, and small businesses fill the gaps below. That density can be overwhelming, but it supports the idea that every square foot has already been claimed and sold by somebody.

#12131D#4D2A79#E62C65#26D9E8#F6DB62

Ghostrunner

A city measured in seconds

Dharma Tower is less about neighborhoods and more about momentum. Long light strips point in the direction of travel, while deep gaps make every successful dash feel risky. The city is vertical because the movement system is vertical too.

How I read a game city

I usually notice these three layers, even when I am not consciously looking for them.

01 / FIRST

Find the contrast

The brightest or most unusual color usually marks the next useful object.

02 / THEN

Read the silhouette

Skylines, gaps, and tall landmarks explain the larger structure of the space.

03 / FINALLY

Follow the rhythm

Repeated ramps, rails, or street turns teach the player how quickly to move.

City vocabulary

A few terms help explain what these settings are doing. Even a small street sign can add another layer to a fictional city.

Cyberpunk

Cyberpunk is a science-fiction genre that puts advanced technology alongside social inequality and powerful corporations. Night City's streets make that contrast hard to miss.

Wayfinding

Wayfinding is the process of working out where you are and how to reach a destination. Mirror's Edge uses color and recognizable shapes to help the player do that while moving.

Reading a sign

A fictional night-market sign could use 夜yoru, meaning “night.” The small reading above the character lets an unfamiliar reader sound it out without replacing the sign's lettering.
